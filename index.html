Privacy Policy

Uneaten · Effective 9 September 2026

The short version. Uneaten keeps everything on your phone. There are no accounts, no sign-in, and no server belonging to us. We cannot see your pantry, your shopping lists, your recipes, or what you spend — that information never reaches us, because there is nowhere for it to go.

Two features reach the internet, and only when you choose to use them. Both are described in full below.

What the app stores, and where

Everything you enter is written to a database file inside the app's own storage on your device:

  • Items in your pantry — name, quantity, unit, storage location, expiry date, tags
  • Shopping lists, the shops you've added, and which items you've ticked off
  • Prices you record, and which shop you bought each item from
  • Recipes you save or import, with their ingredients and steps
  • Records of whether you marked an item as used or wasted
  • Your settings, including expiry thresholds and reminder preferences
  • Barcodes you've scanned, stored alongside the product name so the same scan doesn't need looking up twice

This data stays on your device. It is not uploaded, backed up to us, or shared with anyone. It is included in your device's own backups if you have those switched on — those backups are governed by Apple's or Google's privacy terms, not ours.

Deleting it: "Clear all data" in the app's Settings removes your pantry, lists and recipes. Uninstalling the app removes everything, permanently. We hold no copy, so there is nothing for you to request from us and nothing for us to delete on your behalf.

What leaves your device

Exactly two things, both only when you actively use the feature.

1. Barcode scanning

When you scan a product barcode, the app sends that barcode number to Open Food Facts, a free public food database, and receives the product's name in return. The name is then saved on your device so the same barcode is never looked up twice.

Only the barcode number is sent. No information about you, your device, or anything else in your pantry is included. Open Food Facts will see the request and, as with any internet request, your device's IP address. Their handling of that is covered by their own privacy policy.

Product data from Open Food Facts is used under the Open Database Licence.

If you never scan a barcode, this never happens.

2. Importing a recipe from a link

When you paste a recipe link and choose to import it, the app fetches that page so it can read the recipe from it. This is the same request your web browser would make when visiting the page. The website you are importing from will see the request and your device's IP address.

Only the address you paste is requested. Nothing about you or your data is sent to that site.

If you never import a recipe from a link, this never happens.

Permissions the app asks for

Camera — used solely to read barcodes when you open the scanner. The camera view is not recorded, stored, or transmitted; frames are examined on your device to find a barcode and then discarded. The app never accesses your photo library.

Notifications — used for reminders you switch on yourself: expiring-food alerts, cooking-step timers, and the optional weekly stock-take reminder. These are scheduled and delivered entirely by your own device. The app does not use push notifications, and does not register your device with any notification server. Nothing is sent anywhere to make a reminder appear.

Both permissions are optional. The app works without either; you simply lose the feature that needs it.

What the app does not do

To be explicit, since these are common and we do none of them:

  • No user accounts, sign-in, or profile
  • No analytics, usage tracking, crash reporting, or telemetry of any kind
  • No advertising, and no advertising or tracking SDKs
  • No selling, renting, or sharing of your data — there is no data of yours in our possession to sell
  • No location tracking
  • No access to your contacts, photos, files, or other apps
  • No third-party code that collects information about you

Children

The app is a household food-organisation tool with no social features, no user-generated content shared between people, and no data collection. It is not directed at children specifically, and collects nothing from anyone regardless of age.

Changes to this policy

If the app gains a feature that changes any of the above — cloud sync and backup being the obvious future candidate — this policy will be updated before that feature ships, and the change will be described here rather than quietly amended. Any such feature would be something you opt into.
